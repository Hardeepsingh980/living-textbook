## 2026-10-12 (night): accounts, quizzes, analytics, chapter films, demo pages

Hardeep's six asks: turn the room stills into video; test 30–40 NCERT concepts, have Gemini analyse the videos and produce a printable PDF of the best 20 for the demo; sign in with Google and save progress with analytics; interactive quizzes; BigQuery analytics that help the student improve; a full chapter PDF to a narrated explainer film for teachers.

**Room videos.** Veo (`veo-3.1-fast-generate-001`, image-to-video) animated each green-screen photograph: the hands breathe, the camera sways, the screen stays green. `scripts/compose-phone-video.mjs` tracks the screen's corners in every frame (smoothed), warps the app's own recording onto it with a homography, keys per pixel so fingers stay in front, removes green spill, and plays the background forward then backward so the loop never jumps. The first version copied every pipe chunk (quadratic) and hung; the reader now keeps a chunk list with backpressure.

**Sign-in and progress (Firebase, Firestore, BigQuery).** A Firebase web app was registered for the project; its public config is served by `GET /api/config` from the environment, so no keys are in the repository. Google sign-in uses a popup (redirect when blocked); the server verifies ID tokens with Firebase Admin. On first sign-in, pages made anonymously on that device move to the account. Every lesson reports events (open, step, time in half-minute slices, spoken questions, quiz answers, quiz score, completion) to `POST /api/progress`: a signed-in reader's lesson document in Firestore (`users/{uid}/lessons/{experienceId}`) is updated, and every event, signed in or not, is streamed to BigQuery (`cheerio-app.living_textbook.events`, partitioned by day) and logged to Firebase Analytics. The progress page (`/me`) shows pages, minutes, quiz accuracy, the streak and two weeks of activity; the day-by-day activity, the streak, accuracy by subject and the questions answered wrongly are computed in BigQuery, and Gemini writes three next steps from those numbers (titles and scores from the data only). Tested end to end with a test reader (removed afterwards): three lessons, twelve quiz answers, accuracy 67 %, maths weaker than science, suggestions naming the right pages.

**Quizzes.** `GET /api/experiences/:id/quiz` writes four questions from the page's own text, facts and worked example (one recall, two understanding, one numeric when the page has numbers) and keeps them with the record. The quiz sheet shuffles options per attempt, marks answers at once with the page's reason, offers "Explain it" (the wrong answer goes to the live tutor) and ends with a score; the Quiz pill nudges when the lesson's last step is reached.

**Chapter films for teachers (`/teach`, `POST /api/chapters`).** The PDF is split (up to 40 pages); every page is scored by the quick model for how well it can be brought to life; the best few in book order (similar concepts merged) become lessons through the normal pipeline, two at a time, and each is recorded with the tutor narrating; then a 16:9 film is set: a title card, a card before each part (spoken by Gemini TTS), each lesson in a taped frame beside its key ideas from the page, and closing questions for the class taken from the lessons' quizzes. The first two runs failed at the very end: a segment was written over its own input file.

**What Gemini saw in the recordings, and what changed.** Every chapter page of 26 NCERT science and maths chapters (classes 7–10, 418 pages) was scored by the quick model for how well it can be brought to life; 42 high scorers with distinct concepts were generated, recorded for 45–60 s in presentation mode, and judged by Gemini from video and voice (placement, motion, clarity, correctness, narration, wow, seconds of meaningful motion, verdict). A per-frame motion measure agreed with the judge. Round 1: five pages rated "demo"; motion stopped about 25 s in on most pages, because the moving parts were small and the tutor taught three short steps and waited. Changes: flows along any printed path, bolder and larger motion and five steps in the scene prompt, a tutor that explains each step at more length and demonstrates a what-if itself after the last step. Re-running also exposed a real bug: flow paths were written in figure units, but the normaliser dropped the anchor, so electrons and other flows ran off the figure; paths are now converted to page units (and corrected at render time for scenes already saved), with beads spaced by path length. Rounds 2 and 3 (two runs of every page, current renderer): ten and twelve "demo" verdicts, average "wow" 3.9 → 4.7 → 5.6, seconds of motion per minute 7 → 18 → 25. Generation varies a lot between runs (one circuit page scored 2.2 and then 8.0), and a live demo makes a fresh lesson from the camera every time, so the demo pack ranks pages by their mean over runs. Eight pages were rated demo-ready in every run: the heart's blood flow, distance-time graphs, the V–I graph, Ohm's law, similar polygons by projection, heat conduction along a rod, trigonometric ratios and a basic circuit.

**Security and limits.** Public lesson records no longer carry the owner id (with it, a link holder could list or claim that reader's pages); chapter films are limited to four an hour per visitor.

## 2026-10-11 (evening): reels on Cloud Run, placement third pass

**Reels on Cloud Run (deploys 16–18, commit 40bc4c8).** The first deployed reel came back as 20 s of blank paper with no sound. Reproduced the container locally (Docker build of the same image, probe script against the local server): WebGL via SwiftShader works there, so the image was fine. Recorder diagnostics added (`[reel] … launched in / page loaded in / ready …`, console and page errors, a WebGL probe when the page never renders, and a fail-fast instead of a blank film) showed the real cause on Cloud Run: the browser took 21 s to launch, 21 s to load the page and never reached `assetsReady` in 30 s. Cloud Run only allocates CPU while a request is in flight, and the reel job runs after `POST /api/reels` has returned, so it was starved between the client's polls. Two changes: the service runs on the gen2 execution environment (full Linux for Chromium and ffmpeg), and `GET /api/reels/:id?wait=<ms>` is a long-poll that holds the request open until the job's status or detail changes (up to 50 s); the reel page re-issues it immediately, so the CPU stays on while the page is open. Result on the deployed service: a 30 s reel ready in 79 s, browser launched in 3 s, page ready in 2.6 s, the tutor's voice mixed in (146 audio chunks). `--enable-unsafe-swiftshader` and `--disable-dev-shm-usage` are passed to Chromium as well (recent Chromium needs the opt-in for software WebGL; the container's /dev/shm is small). The always-on CPU option (`--no-cpu-throttling`, about 7 USD a day for one instance) was not taken.

**Placement, third pass (same commit).** The pendulum reel still had its two readouts over the right-hand text column. Cause: stacked labels move as one block, and when no clean spot fits the block the engine gave up and left it where it was. Now a block that fits nowhere is split and each label looks for its own spot; a long readout is tried wrapped at 260 and then 200 units; and as a last resort a blank patch that touches a thin stroke of the figure is taken over printed text when it is clearly cleaner (mean ink ≤ half of the current spot). Re-running the layout on the saved experiences: pendulum readouts from 9 % ink to 0.2 %, refraction and resistors labels (previously kept over print) placed; unit test added. Three pages regenerated and recorded (`eval/output/videos-layout`): readouts in blank figure space or margins on all three.

**Readout precision (same commit).** `{I:0.2}` was read as one decimal (the length of the fraction), so the series-circuit current 0.25 A showed as 0.3 A. `formatDecimals` now reads "0.2" and "0.3" as two and three decimals, and the worked-answer repair widens any format narrower than the page's answer, not only "0.1". The prompt asks for readouts named in plain words as the page names them (no `V_lamp`).

# Living Textbook: demo video script

The video runs 2 minutes 51 seconds. The rules say "strictly under 3 minutes", so do not make it longer when you put your own recordings in.

The video has two acts. Act 1 is the first idea: a science page comes alive on the paper, with a tutor you can talk to. Act 2 is the story: a story page becomes a film you play. Every demo is on a phone. The reference video shows each one as a phone recording in a phone frame, beside the printed page. You record the same thing on your phone and put your clip in its place. The browser appears only once, for the dashboards (progress, chapter films, the developer page).

Every line has a caption. The narrator is the Gemini voice "Sulafat". You can keep it, or record your own voice over the same lines.

## Before you record

1. Print `eval/output/stories/living-textbook-science-page.pdf` (the pendulum page, for videos 1 to 5).
2. Print pages 3 and 4 of `eval/output/stories/living-textbook-story-pages.pdf` in colour (the Ashes story, for videos 6 to 9).
3. Put each page flat on a desk in good light. Avoid glare from a lamp.
4. Sign in on the phone first. A reader who is not signed in can make only 3 lessons a day.
5. Record each phone video in two ways if you can:
   - the phone's own screen recording (Android: Quick Settings, Screen record; iPad: Control Center, Screen Recording). It fits the phone frame in the video;
   - a second phone over your shoulder, in landscape. It shows the paper and the phone together, which proves that this is real. Use it for videos 1, 6 and 9.
6. Keep each take short. The video needs only the seconds given below.

## The shots

### 1. Cold open: the drawing comes alive (0:00 to 0:13)

- **Wow:** the drawing on a printed science page starts to move, on the paper.
- **We see:** the pendulum page and a phone over it. The reader taps "Bring it to life". After about 25 seconds (a note on screen says so), the pendulum swings on the paper with its time readout, and the tutor starts to speak.
- **Narrator:** "This is a page from a class seven science book." / "Point a phone at it, and the drawing comes alive on the paper."
- **Tutor (the app):** "A simple pendulum moves back and forth in what we call an oscillatory motion."
- **Your phone video 1 (13 s):** over your shoulder, the pendulum page. Tap "Bring it to life". Cut the wait. Film the drawing moving on the paper and the tutor's first words.

### 2. Title (0:13 to 0:24)

- **We see:** the title card, with the app in a drawn phone.
- **Narrator:** "This is Living Textbook." / "Science pages become lessons. Story pages become films. All on the paper itself."

### 3. Gemini builds the lesson (0:24 to 0:34)

- **Wow:** you watch Gemini read the page and build the lesson.
- **We see:** the phone while the lesson is made: "Reading the page", pencil marks around the drawing, "Checking it against the page", then the page comes alive. It plays at 2.6 times the speed, and a note on screen says so.
- **Narrator:** "Gemini reads every word, number and picture on the page." / "It builds a live simulation, and checks every number against the page."
- **Your phone video 2 (10 s):** a screen recording of the same steps. Speed it up.

### 4. A tutor you can interrupt (0:34 to 0:57)

- **Wow:** the student cuts in with a question, and the tutor stops and answers at once.
- **We see:** the pendulum swinging on the page. The tutor says "You can watch it swing from one side to the other." The student asks "Wait. What is one oscillation?". The tutor answers: "One oscillation is completed when the bob starts at the mean position, moves to extreme position A, goes to extreme position B, and then returns to the mean position."
- **Narrator:** "A Gemini Live tutor teaches it. Interrupt it any time, with your voice."
- **Your phone video 3 (23 s):** while the tutor explains, ask out loud: "Wait. What is one oscillation?" Film the tutor stopping and answering.

### 5. What if? (0:57 to 1:08)

- **Wow:** a number changes, and the page changes with it.
- **We see:** the string length slider goes to 1.5 m, and the time period on the page goes from 2.01 s to 2.46 s. The tutor: "By increasing the pendulum's length to 1.5 m, the time period becomes longer, meaning each swing takes more time."
- **Narrator:** "Change a number, and the page changes too."
- **Your phone video 4 (11 s):** wait for the tutor's what-if at the end of the lesson, or tap "What if?" and move the slider.

### 6. A quiz from the page (1:08 to 1:15)

- **We see:** the quiz on the phone. One answer is right. One is wrong, and the page's own explanation shows.
- **Narrator:** "Then a quiz, made from the page itself."
- **Your phone video 5 (7 s):** tap "Quiz". Answer one question right and one wrong.

### 7. A story page becomes a film (1:15 to 1:33)

- **Wow:** the printed picture becomes a film on the paper, and the characters speak the book's own words.
- **We see:** the Ashes page and a phone over it. In about 3 seconds the printed picture gets a red outline, a film window grows out of it, and the story starts. The Dog-Spirit says "Take the ashes of the mill, sprinkle them on the withered trees, and they will bloom again."
- **Narrator:** "Story pages come alive too."
- **Your phone video 6 (18 s):** over your shoulder, PDF page 3 of the story PDF. Tap "Bring it to life". Hold still for 3 seconds, then move the phone slowly so that the film is seen to stay on the page.

### 8. You choose what happens (1:33 to 1:49)

- **Wow:** the reader decides what happens next.
- **We see:** the Leader of the Van shouts "Get down on your knees!". The film stops and asks "What will happen when the daimio passes by?", with two choices over the camera. The reader taps "Climb down and hide quietly", and the film shows that choice: "Imagine if the old man climbed down to kneel quietly..."
- **Narrator:** "At the turning point, you choose what happens."
- **Your phone video 7 (16 s):** keep the story playing until it asks the question (about a minute in), then tap one of the two choices.

### 9. Talk to the characters (1:49 to 2:05)

- **Wow:** a character from the book talks back, in its own voice.
- **We see:** "Talk to Dog-Spirit" on the phone. The student asks "Dog-Spirit, why did you help the old man?". The Dog-Spirit answers: "I helped him because he was always so kind to me. And I wanted to bring him joy and wonder, just like he brought to me."
- **Narrator:** "Then talk to the characters. They answer in their own voice."
- **Your phone video 8 (16 s):** tap "Dog-Spirit" while the story plays (or "Talk to Dog-Spirit" at the end) and ask your question out loud.

### 10. Made and checked by Google AI (2:05 to 2:22)

- **Wow:** an AI critic watches every film and sends weak shots back.
- **We see:** a drawn diagram that builds up with the narration, with the Ashes film's own material: the page, the plan, the cast sheet and a drawn frame, a filmed shot playing, the voices, and a real note from the reviewer's first round ("The palanquin glides by itself with no bearers carrying the poles." Shot 5: film it again), with an arrow back to "weak shots made again".
- **Narrator:** "Every film is made by Google AI." / "Gemini plans it, Nano Banana draws it in the book's own style, and Gemini Omni films it." / "Then Gemini watches the film like a critic, and sends weak shots back to be made again."
- Nothing to record.

### 11. The same film for everyone (2:22 to 2:32)

- **Wow:** a second phone, on another page of the same story, gets the same film in seconds.
- **We see:** two phones. Student 1 scans page 62, student 2 scans page 63. Both play the same film after about 3 seconds.
- **Narrator:** "Every page is remembered." / "The next student who scans it, or any other page of the story, gets the same film in seconds."
- **Your phone video 9 (10 s):** two phones over PDF pages 3 and 4 of the story PDF. Tap both at about the same time.

### 12. For classrooms and other apps (2:32 to 2:42)

- **We see:** the web app on a computer: the progress page, a chapter film for the class, and a school's own website with a lesson inside it.
- **Narrator:** "On the web, students see their progress, and teachers make chapter films for the class." / "Other learning apps can add it all with one line of code."
- Nothing to record (the reference uses screen recordings of the web app).

### 13. End card (2:42 to 2:51)

- **We see:** "Any page, alive", the QR code to the scanner, and the Google technology used.
- **Narrator:** "Living Textbook. Any page, alive." / "Built with Gemini on Google Cloud."

## The files

All files are in `eval/output/demo-video-3`, which git ignores.

- `living-textbook-demo-placeholders.mp4`: the reference. A red note on every phone shot says which of your 9 phone videos goes there.
- `living-textbook-demo.mp4`: the same video without the notes.
- `living-textbook-demo-no-narrator.mp4`: the captions, the music and the app's own voices, without the narrator, for your own voice over.
- `narration.wav` and `captions.srt`: the narrator alone, and every caption with its time.
- `timeline.json`: the start and end of every shot.
- The `-small` copies are under 30 MB, for a phone.

To put a recording in, place it over its shot at the times above. A screen recording fits the phone frame (on the 1920 by 1080 picture, the phone screen is 407 by 880 pixels, with its top left corner at 1198, 82). An over-the-shoulder clip can fill the whole shot. Keep the app's own voices (the tutor, the story, the Dog-Spirit) from your recording or from the reference.

## Facts the narration uses

- "About 25 seconds": the pendulum lesson was ready 26 seconds after the tap in the reference recording. On Cloud Run a lesson takes 14 to 29 seconds.
- "In seconds": a known story page finds its film in 2.9 to 3.1 seconds on Cloud Run (measured on 2026-10-18).
- The models: Gemini Flash reads the page and builds the lesson, Gemini Live is the tutor and the characters, Gemini 4 Argon plans and reviews the films, Nano Banana 2.1 draws, Gemini Omni Flash films, Gemini 3.8 TTS speaks, Lyria writes music, and Gemini Embedding 2 remembers the pages.
